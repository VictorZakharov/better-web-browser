# Web Audio implementation status

Breeze implements bounded **offline and live** subsets of the [Web Audio API 1.0 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/). Both contexts render the same graph in the specification's 128-sample-frame quanta. `OfflineAudioContext` returns `Float32` PCM; on Windows, `AudioContext` converts graph output to PCM16 for the contained media worker and XAudio2 device. Tests check actual samples, automation, scheduling, lifecycle, admission, and document teardown; the presence of a constructor alone is not treated as support.

## Available now

- `AudioBuffer` owns 1–32 channels of samples, with `getChannelData`, `copyFromChannel`, and `copyToChannel`.
- `BaseAudioContext.decodeAudioData` detaches its input `ArrayBuffer` synchronously, decodes supported PCM/IEEE-float RIFF/WAVE data on a document-owned worker thread, converts channel samples to `Float32`, resamples to the context rate, and fulfills the promise with an `AudioBuffer` on a media task. Optional success/error callbacks receive the same result or error. Malformed or unsupported encoded data rejects with `EncodingError`; an already detached input rejects with `DataCloneError`.
- `OfflineAudioContext` supports dictionary and positional constructors; `createBuffer`, `createGain`, `createOscillator`, `createBufferSource`, `createConstantSource`, `createStereoPanner`, `createDelay`, `createChannelSplitter`, `createChannelMerger`, `createIIRFilter`, `createBiquadFilter`, `createWaveShaper`, `createPeriodicWave`, `createAnalyser`, and `startRendering`. It can `suspend` at a 128-frame boundary and `resume` after graph edits. Its `destination`, `sampleRate`, `length`, `numberOfChannels`, `currentTime`, `state`, `onstatechange`, and `oncomplete` reflect the rendering lifecycle.
- `AudioContext` shares those graph nodes and creation methods for live output. It starts only after document sticky user activation, accepts an integer `sampleRate` from 8–48 kHz (default 48 kHz), and supplies `resume`, `suspend`, `close`, `state`, `currentTime`, and `statechange`. One unclosed live context is supported per document. A pending `resume()` waits for activation and resolves after the media worker accepts the first PCM chunk and allocates output; `currentTime` advances only for admitted render quanta and remains frozen while suspended. `suspend()` and `close()` settle after the media worker has processed stream closure (or when no worker voice was opened).
- `AudioContext.createMediaStreamSource(stream)` and `MediaStreamAudioSourceNode` can feed a browser-granted microphone track into a live graph. PCM packets enter a bounded ring and are resampled to the context rate; an analyser can read the microphone without connecting it to the speakers. Disabled, stopped, and revoked tracks clear buffered audio. See [capture scope](media-capture.md).
- `GainNode`, `OscillatorNode` (sine, square, sawtooth, triangle), `AudioBufferSourceNode`, and `ConstantSourceNode` contribute actual samples to connected offline graphs. `StereoPannerNode` computes the specification's distinct mono and stereo equal-power equations. `DelayNode` keeps a sample-accurate delay line with a-rate automation, linear interpolation at fractional sample positions, and a tail that survives source completion or a graph edit. `IIRFilterNode` renders immutable feedforward/feedback coefficients with per-channel history and computes complex frequency responses. `BiquadFilterNode` renders all eight specified filter types with a-rate frequency, detune, Q, and gain, and also computes frequency responses. Source `start`/`stop`, buffer offset and duration, simple loop regions, playback rate, detune, and `ended` are implemented.
- `AudioParam` supplies sample-accurate values for these nodes, including set, linear/exponential ramp, target, curve, and cancellation methods. Buffer-source playback rate and detune are fixed to k-rate; other implemented parameters can run at a-rate or k-rate. `connect`/`disconnect` route indexed node outputs to inputs, reject foreign contexts and cycles, and mix multiple inputs. `ChannelSplitterNode` emits one channel per output, padding missing channels with silence; `ChannelMergerNode` keeps its independent mono inputs in positional output channels.
- `createWaveShaper` constructs a `WaveShaperNode` that renders a copied curve with linear interpolation, channel-preserving shaping, and defined edge clamping. The `none` oversample mode works; `2x` and `4x` reject with `NotSupportedError` until their filter chains are implemented.
- `AudioBufferSourceNode` acquires an immutable copy of buffer samples on `start()` or on the first non-null buffer assignment after `start()`. Later `getChannelData()` and `copyToChannel()` writes cannot change that source's scheduled output.
- `PeriodicWave` and `OscillatorNode.setPeriodicWave` render custom Fourier waveforms through band-limited tables and bounded exact harmonic evaluation near a cutoff. Coefficient arrays are copied, with at most 8192 coefficients per wave, a 16 MiB per-context table budget, and a 64-million harmonic-operation render limit. Oscillators retain the built-in sine, square, sawtooth, and triangle types.
- `AnalyserNode` passes PCM through while retaining time-domain samples and computing a Blackman-windowed FFT with smoothing. Its `fftSize` is a power of two from 32 to 32768 (default 2048), with default range -100 to -30 dB and smoothing 0.8; at most 32 analysers may be created per context.
- AudioNode outputs can connect to `AudioParam` inputs. Mono/stereo signals are downmixed to mono, summed with intrinsic automation, sampled at the target parameter's a-rate or k-rate, and clamped at DSP application. Duplicate edges are ignored; disconnect and cross-context, output-index, and cycle validation also apply.
- Offline rendering yields between bounded groups of quanta through an internal, non-cancelable media task. Live rendering produces interleaved stereo PCM16 in chunks of one to seven quanta (128–896 frames), sized near 20 ms at sample rates up to 48 kHz. It retains the exact rendered bytes across admission backpressure, so source and automation state do not advance twice. Navigation discards the owning JavaScript realm and retires its native audio route.

## Deliberate limits and missing behaviors

- The live path currently outputs stereo only, supports at most 48 kHz, and is limited to one unclosed context per document; embedded frames cannot start a live graph. Rates above 48 kHz are rejected because the maximum transport chunk is shorter than the native backpressure retry interval, which would make sustained output unreliable. `AudioWorklet`, media-element sources, non-capture stream sources, `AudioListener`, precise output-latency measurements, and the other processing nodes are unavailable. The default 48 kHz sample rate is a device-independent choice rather than a query of the system output device; device resampling may add latency.
- `decodeAudioData` currently accepts PCM 8/16/24/32-bit and IEEE-float 32-bit RIFF/WAVE, including matching WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE subtypes. MP3, AAC, Ogg, and other encoded formats remain unsupported and reject with `EncodingError`; no support is advertised merely from the method's presence. At most two decode jobs per document, 8 MiB of encoded input per job, and 16 MiB of decoded output per `AudioBuffer` are admitted. The resampler is linear interpolation, not a high-quality reconstruction filter. Navigation cancels document-owned jobs.
- The graph is limited to 256 nodes and 512 connections. An `AudioBuffer` or offline result is limited to 16 MiB; acquired buffer-source content has a 64 MiB per-context budget, automation curves and delay lines each a 32 MiB budget, and WaveShaper curves a 16 MiB budget. Each parameter accepts at most 4096 automation events. An offline render also has a work limit based on frames, channels, and nodes, rechecked when the graph grows after rendering starts. These bounds reject unreasonably large work rather than silently truncating audio.
- Channel mixing currently handles mono replication, mono downmix, and positional multi-channel mapping, with explicit channel layout for splitter/merger ports. It does not yet implement every Web Audio speaker/discrete channel interpretation or `channelCountMode` setting. Feedback cycles through `DelayNode` remain unsupported; the graph rejects all cycles until its renderer can schedule feedback safely.
- Built-in oscillator waveforms and buffer-source resampling are functional but simple. High-quality resampling, remaining dynamic loop edge rules, extreme-value filter edge cases, complete automation-event interactions, and all speaker/discrete channel configurations remain future compatibility work. No claim of full Web Audio or WPT conformance is made.

The implementation follows the [Web Audio rendering model](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#rendering), [offline rendering completion order](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#dom-offlineaudiocontext-startrendering), [AudioParam automation model](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#audioparam), [stereo panning algorithm](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#stereopanner-algorithm), [delay-line model](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#DelayNode), [channel routing](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#ChannelMergerNode), [IIR transfer function](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#IIRFilterNode), and [normative biquad coefficients](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#filters-characteristics). The focused source tests under `src/engine/script/tests/web_audio*.rs` document the currently guaranteed behaviors.

## Native PCM transport

The contained media worker has a separate Web Audio output path wired to the live JavaScript graph. Protocol 1.8 accepts bounded interleaved signed little-endian PCM16: 1–2 channels, 8–192 kHz, and 1–896 frames per chunk (seven 128-frame quanta). The JavaScript destination currently sends stereo. Only one `(document_id, context_id)` stream is admitted per media worker. It uses its own XAudio2 voice and queue, separate from decoded media playback.

The device queue is limited to four buffers and 14 KiB (about 74.7 ms for full stereo chunks at 48 kHz); the renderer handoff allows three waiting chunks plus one in flight. The worker reports `Accepted`, `Backpressure`, or `Rejected`. The renderer retries the *same* chunk after backpressure every 10 ms until accepted or the document is retired; local admission is nonblocking and refuses a full queue. New documents activate the route. Explicit context close cancels retries, skips queued chunks, and closes the worker voice; document cancellation, failure, or shutdown retires the entire route. Worker exit also drops it. Native protocol, output, retry, and silent-worker coexistence tests cover this path.

A fully buffered renderer plus device route can hold roughly 149 ms of stereo audio at 48 kHz before hardware latency; the same four-buffer device queue spans only about 18.7 ms at 192 kHz. These are bounds, not a `latencyHint` or `baseLatency` guarantee. A live graph above two channels would also need downmixing or an expanded native output format. Continuous worker backpressure beyond five seconds faults the graph stream and attempts an identity-scoped close instead of retaining a chunk indefinitely.

The private JavaScript-to-renderer bridge admits one graph chunk locally at a time. The first `accepted` status waits for media-worker output allocation; later statuses mean the bounded renderer handoff owns their chunks, not that hardware has played them. Worker backpressure is retried by the renderer with the same bytes; JavaScript retries only a locally refused chunk and waits for an accepted/rejected status before progressing. A later worker/device fault rejects the next chunk and suspends the context. Four chunks are primed, then subsequent chunks are paced against a monotonic deadline. `suspend()` retires the current stream, retaining any unaccepted rendered chunk for a fresh, monotonically increasing stream ID on resume. `close()` and document retirement cancel retries; close acknowledgement waits for worker processing so an in-flight admission cannot leave a stale voice playing.
